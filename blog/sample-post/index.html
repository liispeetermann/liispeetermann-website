Warming foods for the colder months

24 November 2026 · 6 min read

← All articles

As the days grow shorter, many of us feel heavier, colder and more tired. It isn’t a lack of willpower. Your body is responding to the season, and it is asking for something different.

This is placeholder text for your article. In Tibetan medicine, winter is the time when cold and heavy qualities naturally build up in the body. Digestion can slow down, energy can dip, and we may find ourselves reaching for comfort without quite knowing why.

The good news is that small, steady changes make a real difference. You don’t need a strict plan or special ingredients. You need warmth, rhythm and a little attention to how food makes you feel.

Why warmth matters in winter

Cold, raw food and iced drinks ask the body to work harder to stay warm. Cooked meals, warming spices and hot drinks support digestion instead of competing with it.

Your body is not asking for more discipline. It is asking for warmth.

Simple ways to begin

A few places to start this week:

  • Start the day warm. A cup of hot water or ginger tea before breakfast.
  • Choose cooked over raw. Soups, stews and roasted vegetables are easier to digest in the cold months.
  • Use warming spices. Ginger, cinnamon, cardamom and black pepper in everyday meals.
  • Eat at regular times. A steady rhythm helps digestion as much as what is on the plate.

Listening to your own body

Everyone is different, and what warms one person may feel too heavy for another. Notice how you feel an hour or two after eating: settled and energised, or tired and bloated? That is useful information, and it is the start of understanding what your body needs.
